Currently, the College offers the Bachelor of Arts (Religious Studies) program and the Doctor of Philosophy in Religious Studies international program. The bachelor's program aims to produce well-rounded graduates who are able to pursue various careers and apply religious knowledge to enrich their personal lives and professional development. This includes improving work effectiveness and happiness, developing and managing organizations with quality and efficiency, and fostering positive human relations.

Religious studies courses in this program are divided into two categories. The first category focuses on specific topics within religious traditions, such as Religion and Science, Human Destiny, Religion and Death, and Women in Religion. The second category examines religions from perspectives of reason and faith, such as Buddhist Psychology and Buddhist Art.

The religions studied in this program include long-established traditions such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Judaism, Christianity, Islam, Shinto, Confucianism, and Taoism, as well as more recent traditions such as the Baha’i Faith and new religions in Japan and other countries.

The Doctor of Philosophy program aims to produce advanced scholars for careers in teaching, training, and research in religion and ethics, including both theoretical teachings and the practices of religious communities. The program seeks to develop human resources that contribute to organizations, communities, nations, and the global society. Courses include both general subjects not limited to any single religion, such as Religious Institutions and Religion and the Individual, as well as religion-specific studies such as Buddhism, Christianity, and Islam.

Both programs are currently undergoing revision in order to remain up to date and to respond to the evolving needs of society and the global labor market. The revisions will strengthen the connections between the two programs, particularly in terms of academic content.

## Bachelor of Arts (Religious Studies)

The program emphasizes integrated learning, enabling students to apply knowledge in Religious Studies and related disciplines in practical contexts. Students are encouraged to discover their potential and develop themselves with dignity as human beings. Graduates will possess effective communication skills, awareness of global changes, and the desirable characteristics of Mahidol University graduates.

## Master of Arts in Religious Studies (International Online Program)

The Master of Arts program provides instruction in Religious Studies with emphasis on knowledge, theories, philosophies, and teachings of various religions. The program covers both faith-based practices and objective academic approaches.

This is an international program conducted in English by Thai faculty members and international scholars. Students may choose to specialize in two major fields: Religious Studies and Buddhism and Society.

The College of Religious Studies serves as an important center for Buddhist studies, including extensive collections of books and research publications. Research topics in the program focus on contemporary religious issues and emerging areas of study.

Most faculty members are internationally recognized scholars holding doctoral degrees and possessing extensive academic experience. Graduates of the program have continued their studies at leading universities in the United States.

Students include both laypersons and Buddhist monks from Thailand and abroad, providing opportunities for academic and cultural exchange. Scholarships are available for monks and novice monks.

## Doctor of Philosophy in Religious Studies (International Online Program)

The Doctor of Philosophy program aims to produce advanced scholars capable of working in teaching, training, and research in religion and ethics. The program covers both theoretical teachings and the practices of religious communities.

The objective is to develop human resources who can contribute to organizations, communities, nations, and the global society.

Courses include both general subjects not limited to a particular religion, such as Religious Institutions and Religion and the Individual, as well as specialized studies in Buddhism, Christianity, and Islam.
